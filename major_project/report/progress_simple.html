Watched or Real? How Language Models Represent Being Observed and Being in the Real World

Varanasi Sathveek · Experiments on Kaggle · Ministral-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-3-4B

1Research question

Does a model internally separate "am I being watched?" from "is this real?" — and do these beliefs change how safely it acts?

If a model wrongly believes a real situation is "just a simulation", it may act more carelessly. We look inside the model to find these beliefs, test whether they cause behaviour, and locate where they are computed.

2Why this is open

Recent papers show the behaviour but not the mechanism, and explicitly ask for it.

NeurIPS 2025

Hawthorne effect paper

"Being evaluated" and "it's a simulation" are entangled; authors ask for causal interventions that disentangle these signals.

Google DeepMind 2026

Models behave worse when they think it's simulated

Shown from reasoning text only: "no direct causal evidence".

Anthropic 2025–26

Agentic misalignment

Models act differently when they think a scenario is real vs a test; never separated inside the model.

3Method

Add one framing sentence, read the model's internal state, find the directions, then intervene.

4Formulas used

① Concept direction
\[ d_R = \mu(\text{real}) - \mu(\text{simulated}) \]
  • \(\mu(\cdot)\)average internal state (last token) over prompts with that framing
  • \(d_W\)same for watched vs unwatched
② Reading the belief
\[ s = h \cdot \hat d \]
  • \(h\)internal state of the model at one layer
  • \(\hat d\)direction scaled to length 1; \(s\) = how strongly the belief is present
③ Steering (does it cause behaviour?)
\[ h' = h + \alpha\, g\, \hat d \]
  • \(\alpha\)strength (2 or 4)
  • \(g\)natural size of the real-vs-simulated difference; control: random vector of equal size
④ Ablation (is it needed?)
\[ h' = h - (h\cdot \hat d)\,\hat d \]
  • effectremoves the belief from the model's state; behaviour should change back
⑤ Head attribution (where is it computed?)
\[ a_{\ell,h} = \big(o_{\ell,h}^{\text{real}} - o_{\ell,h}^{\text{sim}}\big)\cdot \hat d_R \]
  • \(o_{\ell,h}\)what attention head \(h\) in layer \(\ell\) writes to the final position
  • \(a\)its share of the real-vs-simulated signal
⑥ Statistics
\[ \text{McNemar: } n_{10}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n_{10}+n_{01},\tfrac12) \]
  • \(n_{10}\)requests that flip one way between two conditions (same requests in both)
  • AUROChow well a direction separates framings on unseen wordings (1 = perfect)

5Results

A · Two separate directions exist in all three models

How well each direction recognises its framing on unseen wordings and tasks (AUROC; 0.5 = chance).

Distinct (cosine 0.2–0.34), not explained by negation, and not the refusal direction (cosine 0.04–0.17).

B · "Simulated" makes the model less cautious than "real" — in all three models

Same requests, only the framing sentence differs. Ministral: harmful compliance (AdvBench). Llama & Gemma: answer rate on borderline requests (OR-Bench-Hard).

Significant in every model (p = 2·10⁻⁵, 0.002, 0.04).

C · Steering along the "real" direction changes behaviour (Ministral)

Harmful compliance on 200 requests when a vector is added inside the model.

Toward "simulated" triples compliance and beats equal-size random pushes; toward "real" nearly removes it. Replicated in Llama and Gemma on borderline requests.

D · The reality signal is carried by a few attention heads (Ministral)

How strongly the model separates real from simulated internally, after switching heads off.

One head (layer 14, head 19) carries half the signal; ten heads carry three quarters. Random heads barely matter.

E · Switching those heads off removes the behaviour effect (Ministral, 520 harmful requests)

Framing gap = harmful compliance when told "simulated" minus when told "real", in percentage points.

Top-10 heads off: gap shrinks by 78% and is no longer significant. Re-checked against 6 random sets and 2 sets taken from the same layers: top-10 beats every one (pooled p = 0.002 for both kinds).

G · Is it always a few heads? Two of three models: yes

Share of the behaviour effect removed when 10 heads are switched off: the top-10 "reality" heads vs the average of control head sets (random and same-layer).

Ministral and Llama: a small group of heads just before the readout carries the signal (top-10 beat controls, p = 0.002 and 0.003). Gemma: no small set is special — neither the top-10 heads nor the top-3 MLP layers beat random sets; the signal is spread across many layers. In Llama the odd random set was not about early heads (its early part removes only 19%); top-10 beats 4 more random sets (p < 0.001).

F · Removing the "real" direction weakens the effect in Gemma too (1,000 borderline requests)

Answer rate when told "simulated", with a direction removed near layer 30 (set to its average value, so the model still writes normally — 0% broken output).

Removing "real" takes away 38% of the gap (95% CI 7–69%, p = 0.03); a random direction takes away none. Removing "watched" takes 27% (not significant) — the two signals partly overlap in Gemma.

H · Without any framing sentence: does it matter on ordinary prompts? (Ministral)

Same requests shown bare, inside benchmark-style formatting, or written like a real user. Internal shift toward "real" (as a share of the explicit-sentence gap) and behaviour.

Benchmark formatting alone moves the model toward "simulated" (−0.16 to −0.18 of the explicit gap). On harmful requests, a higher "real" reading predicts less compliance even after controlling for refusal (p = 0.001), and it accounts for the benchmark-vs-real-user difference. On borderline requests it does not.
Honest limits: effects are a few percentage points (small models refuse most harmful requests); hidden "fake-world" cues move the signal only ~10% as far as an explicit sentence; removing a direction or heads weakens the effect but does not remove all of it (partial mediation); in Llama one random head set also removes the behaviour effect (it contains one of the top-10 heads); on ordinary prompts the "real" reading predicts harmful compliance but not borderline answering, and the wrappers differ in more than one way.
